# 0003: Real 3D in the engine: depth, meshes, and a 3D camera

- **Status:** accepted, 2026-10-15. Planned; nothing is built yet.
- **Asked by:** Sonnet of Melody, whose menu is to become a 3D world (its `docs/ART-DIRECTION.md`): "very dynamic, so like 3d world and stuff... big and ambitious". Asked to choose between real 3D and faking it as the cinematic does, the player chose real 3D.

## Context

The engine draws flat. What there is to build on:

- **The RHI** (`engine/rhi`, section 10.1) has one render pass, to the swapchain: one color attachment, cleared and stored, no depth. On Vulkan (1.1) that's a `VkRenderPass` made in `VulkanRenderDevice::CreateSwapchainRenderPass` and one framebuffer per swapchain image (`VulkanSwapchain::CreateFramebuffers`). Pipelines (`PipelineDesc`) have no depth state, cull nothing, take counter-clockwise as front, and use one sample. Their data is push constants alone (128 bytes, the minimum every device gives) and textures at set 0; there are no uniform buffers. Blending is opaque or straight alpha.
- **The renderer** (`engine/renderer`) has `SpriteBatch`, whose `DrawQuad` draws any convex quadrilateral, and `Camera2D`, whose `ClipTransform` carries the swapchain's pre-rotation (Android) as a cosine and sine. Section 10.3 always listed a `Camera3D` "used by the Highway mode", never built.
- **Shaders** are Slang, compiled by `hyoshi_add_shaders` (`cmake/Shaders.cmake`) to SPIR-V for Vulkan and WGSL for WebGPU; WebGL 2's GLSL is written by hand.
- **The frame:** scenes draw into the sprite batch while they run (`Application::RunScene`); `Application::RenderFrame` begins the pass, flushes text, ends the sprite batch, renders the debug UI, and ends the pass.
- **3D so far is faked on the CPU:** Sonnet of Melody's cinematic opening (`CinematicScene`'s `View` projects points) and its 3D lane track (`LaneGameplayScene`) project corners and draw flat shapes. That holds for a lake of triangles; a world built that way runs into overlap and sorting, textures that warp across big quads, and no lighting.

## Decision

1. **Depth comes to the RHI**, Vulkan first. The swapchain render pass gets a depth attachment; pipelines get depth, culling, and winding settings, defaulting to what every pipeline does now, so nothing drawn today changes.
2. **A 3D renderer sits beside `SpriteBatch`**: meshes, materials, a perspective camera, simple lighting, and a draw list that the app flushes before the 2D, which draws over it.
3. **Push constants carry each draw at first**; uniform buffers wait until a scene needs more lights than one.
4. **Where the RHI can't do depth (WebGPU and WebGL 2, for now), it says so** (`IRenderDevice::HasDepth`), and games keep a 2D path there. Sonnet of Melody's old look is that path.
5. **Stand-in shapes are built in code** until models come from Blender (glTF, later).

## Milestones

### 1. Depth in the RHI (Vulkan)

- **The attachment.** A depth image the swapchain's size, made and remade with it (`CreateSwapchainResources`), in the first format the device can attach of `D32_SFLOAT`, `D24_UNORM_S8_UINT`, `D16_UNORM` (`vkGetPhysicalDeviceFormatProperties`, optimal tiling, `DEPTH_STENCIL_ATTACHMENT`). Cleared to 1 on load, not stored (`DONT_CARE`): tile-based GPUs then never write it out. Usage `DEPTH_STENCIL_ATTACHMENT | TRANSIENT_ATTACHMENT`, with lazily allocated memory where the device has it (VMA's `GPU_LAZILY_ALLOCATED`), so it costs phones next to nothing.
- **The render pass** gets the attachment and its reference; the subpass dependency adds the early and late fragment test stages and depth writes, since one depth image serves both frames in flight. Framebuffers take two attachments; `BeginRenderPass` clears two values.
- **`PipelineDesc`** gains `DepthMode Depth` (`None`, the default; `Test`; `TestAndWrite`), `CullMode Cull` (`None`, the default; `Back`; `Front`), `FrontFace Front` (`CounterClockwise`, the default; `Clockwise`), and `BlendMode::Additive` (source alpha, one), for light and magic. Every Vulkan pipeline now carries a depth-stencil state, which a render pass with depth requires; `None` turns the test off.
- **`IRenderDevice::HasDepth()`**: true on Vulkan; false on WebGPU and WebGL 2, whose `CreatePipeline` refuses a depth mode until they have it.
- Screenshots copy the color image, as now.

### 2. The 3D renderer (`engine/renderer`)

- **Meshes.** `MeshVertex`: position (3 floats), normal (3 floats), texture coordinates (2 floats), color (4 bytes), 36 bytes. `CreateMesh(MeshData)` uploads vertices and 32-bit indices once to GPU-only buffers and returns a handle.
- **`MeshBuilder`**, for shapes in code: boxes, cylinders, planes subdivided (to bend, as a book's pages), with normals and texture coordinates. Faces wind counter-clockwise seen from outside.
- **`Camera3D`**: position, target, up, vertical field of view, near and far. Its projection is `glm::perspectiveRH_ZO` (depth 0 to 1, as Vulkan's) with y flipped (Vulkan's clip space points down), then the swapchain's pre-rotation applied to clip x and y (from `Camera2D`'s `ClipTransform::Rotation`), its aspect from `GetLogicalExtent`. The y flip mirrors winding, so the mesh pipelines take clockwise as front and cull the back.
- **Materials**: a base color, a texture (a 1x1 white one when none), emission, and unlit.
- **Lighting in 128 bytes of push constants**: the object-to-clip matrix (64 bytes, sent as four columns so that glm's and Slang's matrix layouts can't disagree), the light's direction in object space (16), the camera's position in object space (16, for a rim light and fog), the base color (16), and the rest (16: emission, ambient, rim, fog). One directional key light, a two-color ambient (sky and ground), a rim, and emission. Moving the light and camera into object space on the CPU works for the rotations, moves, and uniform scales a world of props uses. Candles glow by emission and additive sprites until milestone 5's lights.
- **The draw list.** A scene submits (mesh, transform, material) while it runs; `RenderFrame` flushes it after `BeginRenderPass` and before `spriteBatch.End`: opaque draws front to back, then transparent ones back to front (alpha, depth tested, not written), then additive ones. The 2D draws over the 3D. A 2D background under the 3D, later, would draw the sprite layers below a line first.
- **How scenes reach it:** a `renderer::Renderer3D&` in `AppServices`.
- **The shader:** `shaders/Mesh.slang`, through `hyoshi_add_shaders`, SPIR-V only for now.

### 3. A test scene (in the game)

Sonnet of Melody's "Book (3D)": a book open on a lectern in a dark room, with candles, its pages textured with staff lines drawn into a texture on the CPU; it turns slowly, and a drag orbits it. Reached from Home in Developer mode (a "3D test" door, as Song list is) and with `HYOSHI_BOOK=1`. Checked on the Mac (MoltenVK), Windows, and Android (the Fold: the frame rate, and the pre-rotation in both orientations).

### 4. The game's menu as a 3D world

The game's part (its `docs/ART-DIRECTION.md`): a new menu scene beside today's `MenuScene`, which the old look and devices without depth keep.

### 5. Later, as the world needs it

- **Multisampling (4x)**: the render pass and every pipeline's sample count, resolving to the swapchain. 3D edges alias without it; tile-based GPUs do it nearly free.
- **A uniform buffer per frame** (a descriptor set beside the textures'), for many lights (every candle) and fog.
- **glTF models** from Blender (cgltf, a single-header library), with their textures.
- **Render targets** (also the editor's milestone 2, decision 0002), then bloom for the magic, and shadows.
- **Depth on the web**: WebGPU (its WGSL comes from Slang for free), then WebGL 2 (GLSL by hand).

## Consequences

- Every Vulkan pipeline has a depth-stencil state; existing ones keep depth off, so 2D draws as before.
- One depth image per swapchain: on desktops a little memory; on phones, with lazy allocation, about none.
- Games must allow for devices without depth: `HasDepth`.
- Until milestone 5, the world has one light; warm candlelight is emission and glow, not lit surfaces.
- Without multisampling, 3D edges will show stairs on desktops; plan milestone 5's first item early if they bother.
